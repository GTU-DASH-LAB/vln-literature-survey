What “zero-shot” means — five different claims

The term carries at least five distinct meanings in this literature, and papers routinely compare results across them. Auditing which claim each paper actually makes — and what its components were nonetheless trained on — is the original contribution of Section IX.

ZS-1

Task-level / training-free

No training on any VLN dataset. An off-the-shelf LLM or VLM is prompted at inference time. The strongest claim, and the rarest one that survives inspection.

Audit: almost every “training-free” agent still leans on a waypoint predictor, an open-vocabulary detector, or CLIP weights.
ZS-2

Open-vocabulary goal

The agent handles object or goal categories never seen during training. This is what zero-shot ObjectNav almost always means.

Audit: “unseen category” is relative to the navigation policy, not to the vision backbone that has seen the web.
ZS-3

Cross-dataset transfer

Trained on one benchmark, evaluated on another with no fine-tuning — R2R → REVERIE, or discrete → continuous.

Audit: check whether the source and target share the same underlying scene dataset (both on Matterport3D is a weak transfer claim).
ZS-4

Unseen environment

New scenes, same task and same instruction distribution.

Audit: the standard val-unseen split is not zero-shot. Several papers present it as though it were.
ZS-5

Cross-embodiment / sim-to-real

The policy is applied to a robot, morphology or sensor suite it was never trained on.

Audit: often the honest reason a real-robot demo is zero-shot is that no real-world training data exists.

Databases — and what to leave out

The decisive coverage fact: Scopus and Web of Science do not index NeurIPS, ICLR, ICML or CoRL (PMLR and OpenReview are outside both). For a 2023–2026 foundation-model topic, omitting arXiv and OpenReview does not make the review conservative — it makes it incomplete.

What to read first

A survey gets written from about a hundred papers, not from two thousand. This is that hundred, drawn from the pre-screened corpus and grouped by the chapter each one serves. Two choices shape it and both belong in Sec. III: citations are divided by age, because ranking on raw counts ranks 2024 rather than the field; and the tier quotas are ceilings, not targets — a tier that cannot fill itself from peer-reviewed or cited work is left short, because that shortfall says something the survey should report.

Screening & criteria

Record the number at every arrow — those numbers are the PRISMA figure. Single-author screening is a validity threat: re-screen a random 10 % sample after a week and report intra-rater agreement.

Inclusion

  1. IC1Published 2023-01-01 – 2026-09-30
  2. IC2Peer reviewed, or a preprint meeting the rule below
  3. IC3English, full text obtainable
  4. IC4Agent navigates a 3D environment from visual observations
  5. IC5Behaviour is conditioned on natural language — instruction, goal description, dialogue, or an open-vocabulary object name
  6. IC6Contributes a method, dataset, system or empirical study

Exclusion

  1. EC1Language-free navigation (PointNav, pure geometric SLAM)
  2. EC2Vision-free “navigation” (text-only graph agents)
  3. EC3Manipulation-only VLA work
  4. EC4Non-embodied vision-language work
  5. EC5Autonomous driving — decide once, state it explicitly
  6. EC6Surveys (collected separately for Table I)
  7. EC7Abstracts under 4 pages, posters, unevaluated reports
  8. EC8Duplicate versions — keep the most complete
Preprint rule — write this verbatim into Section III, because reviewers will ask. An arXiv-only paper is included if it meets IC4–IC6 and at least one of: (a) ≥ 10 citations on Semantic Scholar, (b) it introduces a benchmark already used by other included papers, or (c) it reports state of the art on a standard benchmark. Every such entry is marked “preprint” in the master table.
Zero-shot boundary — zero-shot ObjectNav papers are included: an open-vocabulary object category is language conditioning under IC5, and excluding them would gut Section IX. Tag them task=ObjectNav so their success rates are never averaged with R2R success rates.

How this page was built. Seed works were resolved against the OpenAlex API for venue, DOI and open-access status, then re-counted through the Semantic Scholar batch endpoint. Both steps are scripted and repeatable.
